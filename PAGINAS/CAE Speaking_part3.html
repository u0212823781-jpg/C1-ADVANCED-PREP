Collaborative Task

Discuss a question with your partner using the five written prompts. Exchange and evaluate ideas for about 2 minutes. Then negotiate a decision together for about 1 minute.

01

02

Build a Stronger Contribution

Basic

"Voluntary work is useful."

Better

"Voluntary work is useful because young people can develop practical skills."

Developed C1 Contribution

"Voluntary work could be particularly valuable because it exposes young people to real responsibilities while allowing them to develop interpersonal skills. It may also make them more aware of the needs of others."

Evaluate Explain Develop Involve
Develop your point, then create an opportunity for your partner to respond.

Prioritise and Compare

Of the two, this seems more practical.

You do not need to discuss every prompt in equal detail. Explore relevant ideas, respond to your partner and keep the conversation moving.
04

Separate Answers or Real Interaction?

Two Separate Monologues
A

"Having a part-time job is useful because young people can earn money."

B

"Voluntary work is useful because young people can help others."

  • The candidates present unrelated answers.
  • Neither responds to the other.
  • No comparison or development takes place.
A Collaborative Discussion
A

"A part-time job could be valuable because it gives young people a sense of responsibility. What's your view?"

B

"I agree to some extent, although voluntary work might develop similar skills without the pressure of earning money."

A

"That's true. Perhaps the main difference is that paid work teaches financial independence, whereas volunteering may encourage greater social awareness."

B

"That's a good point. Which of those benefits do you think would have the greater long-term impact?"

Listen Respond Develop Invite
Part 3 is a conversation, not a sequence of individual speeches.

Guided Practice

Discussion Phase · About 2 Minutes
How might these experiences prepare young people for adult life?
Having a part-time job
Doing voluntary work
Completing a challenging creative project
Taking part in a team activity
Studying or travelling abroad

Six Steps to a Strong Discussion

1
Introduce

Choose a relevant prompt and present an initial idea.

2
Evaluate

Consider its benefits, limitations or consequences.

3
Respond

React directly to your partner's contribution.

4
Connect

Compare or link different prompts.

5
Involve

Invite your partner to add another perspective.

6
Move On

Introduce another prompt when appropriate.

Sample Discussion
A

"Having a part-time job could be particularly useful because it teaches young people to manage their time and take responsibility for their own work. Would you agree?"

B

"To some extent, yes, although studying abroad might have an even greater impact. Young people would have to become independent while adapting to an unfamiliar environment."

A

"That's a fair point. Both experiences encourage independence, but in rather different ways. A job involves meeting professional expectations, whereas living abroad requires considerable personal adaptability."

B

"Exactly. Voluntary work could also be valuable because it combines practical experience with a greater awareness of other people's needs."

A

"I hadn't considered that. It might develop empathy as well as communication skills. What about taking part in a team activity?"

B

"That could teach cooperation, although its long-term impact might depend on how much responsibility the person is actually given."

06

Reaching a Decision

Decision Phase · About 1 Minute

Now decide which two experiences would have the greatest long-term impact on a young person.

A

"On balance, I'd be inclined to choose having a part-time job and studying abroad. Both would require young people to become genuinely independent."

B

"I agree about studying abroad, but I wonder whether voluntary work might have a broader impact than a part-time job, particularly in terms of empathy and social awareness."

A

"That's a valid point. However, a job may prepare someone more directly for adult responsibilities, such as managing money and meeting professional expectations."

B

"True. In that case, could we settle on studying abroad and having a part-time job?"

A

"Yes, I'd be happy with those two because they combine personal independence with practical preparation for working life."

Suggest Compare Reconsider Negotiate Conclude
A decision should emerge from the discussion. Do not simply announce an answer at the beginning.

Key Takeaways
  • Use all five prompts as support, not as a checklist.
  • Respond to what your partner actually says.
  • Develop ideas instead of giving a series of short opinions.
  • Keep the discussion balanced.
  • Treat the decision as a separate task.
  • Negotiate a decision, but do not force artificial agreement.

Interaction Check

Listen

Did I respond to what my partner actually said?

Develop

Did I support my ideas with reasons, examples or consequences?

Connect

Did I compare or link different prompts?

Evaluate

Did I consider advantages, limitations or long-term effects?

Involve

Did I give my partner opportunities to contribute?

Negotiate

Did we compare alternatives before reaching a decision?

Decision Question

Listen actively. Build on ideas. Negotiate rather than compete.
