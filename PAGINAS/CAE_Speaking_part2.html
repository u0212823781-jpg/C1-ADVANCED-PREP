Long Turn

You will receive three photographs. Choose two, compare them and answer both questions. Speak for 1 minute without interruption; then your partner responds for about 30 seconds.

02

Choose Two — Then Connect Them

Compare two of the photographs and say what challenges the people might be facing and how successfully they might deal with them.

A
Mountain rescue team helping an injured person
B
Surgical team carrying out an operation
C
Musician performing live on stage
  1. 1What challenges might the people be facing?
  2. 2How successfully might they deal with them?
03

Describe or Compare?

Listing details
  • Describes each photograph separately.
  • Lists only what is visible.
  • Gives ideas without connecting them.
  • Does not address both questions.
"In the first photograph, there are several people on a mountain. In the second, there are some doctors in an operating theatre."
Comparing & interpreting
  • Selects two photographs and connects them.
  • Highlights relevant similarities and differences.
  • Speculates about challenges and possible outcomes.
  • Answers both questions clearly.
"Both situations involve considerable pressure, although the rescue team is dealing with unpredictable external conditions, whereas the surgical team is working in a highly controlled environment."
Focus on connections, meaning and interpretation — not simply on what you can see.
04

Build a C1 Answer

1
Select

Choose two photographs and identify their common theme.

2
Compare

Connect the situations through relevant similarities and differences.

3
Speculate

Suggest reasons, feelings, challenges or possible outcomes.

4
Evaluate

Address the second question and reach a balanced conclusion.

Model Answer

Both the mountain rescue team and the surgical team are operating in extremely demanding situations where people's safety may depend on their decisions. The rescue workers are likely to be facing unpredictable weather and difficult terrain, whereas the surgeons are working in a more controlled environment. Nevertheless, both groups must remain calm, communicate clearly and react quickly if anything goes wrong. The rescue team may find the physical conditions particularly challenging, while the surgeons are probably under intense psychological pressure because even a minor mistake could have serious consequences. As for how successfully they might deal with these challenges, both groups appear to be highly trained and accustomed to working as a team. On balance, I'd expect them to cope effectively, although the rescuers may have less control over the situation.

05

One-Minute Strategy

A suggested pacing strategy for practice — not an official Cambridge timing division.

0–10 sec
Open clearly

State which two photographs you have chosen and identify the shared context.

10–40 sec
Develop the comparison

Compare the situations while addressing the first question.

40–50 sec
Address the second question

Evaluate, speculate or explain how the people might respond.

50–60 sec
Conclude naturally

Give a balanced final judgement without repeating everything.

Key Takeaways
  • Strong answers connect ideas rather than list details.
  • Good speakers speculate and support their interpretations.
  • Both parts of the task must be addressed.
  • The answer should remain coherent for the full minute.
07

Your Partner's 30 Seconds

Listen carefully while your partner speaks. Afterwards, the examiner will ask you a short question related to your partner's photographs.

Examiner's Question

Which situation do you think requires the greatest concentration?

Model Response

I'd say the operation probably requires the greatest concentration because even a very small mistake could have immediate and serious consequences. However, the rescue workers would also need to remain extremely focused, particularly if the weather or terrain were unpredictable.

About 30 seconds Answer directly, justify your choice and add one relevant comparison.
